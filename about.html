The back of the package, without the scavenger hunt.

I kept looking up the same foods — rice, oats, lentils, milk — and digging through ad-heavy pages with inconsistent serving sizes. Pantry Index is one place to search a food, pick a real portion, and see the whole day add up.

How to use it

  1. Search. Type a food (“banana”, “eggs”, “dal”). Everyday staples rank first; press / to jump to search and Enter to open the top match.
  2. Pick a portion. Each food offers its own USDA portions — 1 medium banana, 1 slice of bread, 1 breast, 1 cup, 1 tbsp — plus grams and ounces. Fractions like 1/2 or 1 1/2 work.
  3. Add it to a meal. Your day totals update instantly: calories, protein, carbs, fat and fiber against your goals, with vitamins, minerals and a full label one tap away.
  4. Come back tomorrow. Each day is saved separately in this browser. Use ‹ › to look back, or copy a previous day to start from it.

Where the numbers come from

All 7,793 foods from USDA’s SR Legacy database, plus 64 newer Foundation and Survey (FNDDS) records for common staples. Values are per 100 g from the source; portions are USDA’s measured gram weights for that exact food. Each food links to its USDA record.

Some newer Foundation records only report a few nutrients. Where that happens, the missing nutrients (never the reported ones) are filled from the closest SR Legacy record, and the food’s details say so.

Honest gaps

  • A dash (—) means the source didn’t report that nutrient. Totals marked ≥ are a known minimum because some foods in the day lack that value.
  • Added sugars, iodine and biotin are left out: USDA reports them for almost no foods.
  • Raw, dry, cooked, canned and frozen are separate records because their nutrition differs. Pick the state you actually weigh or eat.
  • Daily Values are general FDA references, not personal targets. This is a reference tool, not medical advice.
